# SQL analyses

There are 14 queries in `sql/analysis/`. Each file starts with a header that gives:

* the business question
* the exact definition
* how to interpret the result
* its limitations
* a validation check you can run to confirm the numbers

All 14 read only the analytics marts and warehouse, never the hospital tables. They run unchanged in `psql` as `hms_analytics`:

```bash
psql -U hms_analytics -d hms -f sql/analysis/04_not_completed_weekday_hour.sql
```

`npm run sql:check` runs every query read-only, with `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)`, and writes the plans to [sql-plans/](sql-plans/README.md).

> **Example results below are from the synthetic demo database** (fictional data generated by `npm run synthetic`, seed 42), measured on 29 October 2026. They show what each query produces. They are not findings about any real hospital. Some patterns, such as the higher not-completed rate for patients with earlier misses, exist only because the generator was written to include them.

## The queries

| # | Question | SQL techniques | Example result (synthetic) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | How is volume changing month on month and year on year? | CTE, `LAG` and `LAG(…, 12)` window functions, `NULLIF` | Year-on-year growth for complete months in 2026 ranged from +5% to +18%. The current and future months are partial and flagged as such. |
| 02 | Which departments carry the most demand each quarter, and is their share moving? | `SUM() OVER (PARTITION BY)`, `DENSE_RANK`, `LAG` per department | In Q4 2026 so far, General Medicine had 23.1% of appointments, ahead of Pediatrics (14.3%) and Orthopedics (10.4%). |
| 03 | What is the smoothed weekly trend per department? | Named `WINDOW` frames (4 and 13 weeks), `ROWS BETWEEN`, rule-based trend signal | Gives rising, steady or falling per department and week. `weeks_in_window` marks the first 12 weeks, which have short windows. |
| 04 | At which weekday and hour are appointments most often not completed? | Conditional aggregation, 95% Wilson score interval written in SQL | Monday 15:00 was 19.7% (95% CI 15.9–24.2%), against 12.0% overall: reliably worse than average. |
| 05 | Do patients come back after their first visit? | Cohorts from `MIN() OVER`, `AGE()` month arithmetic, retention matrix with suppression | About 55–61% (54.6% to 60.8%) of the October 2024 cohort had a visit in each of the next six months. |
| 06 | How long between a patient's visits, by department? | `LAG` per patient, `PERCENTILE_CONT` median and 90th percentile | Median gap 16–18 days in every department, 90th percentile 90–105 days. |
| 07 | How full are doctors' weeks, and how often are they near full? | Two CTE aggregations joined, weekly utilisation, `PERCENTILE_CONT`, share of capacity back-filled | Utilisation ranged from 33% to 80% by doctor. The busiest doctor was at 90% or more in 41% of weeks. 99.8% of capacity is back-filled, because the pipeline was new. |
| 08 | Is workload concentrated on a few doctors in each department? | `ROW_NUMBER` and a Gini coefficient computed in SQL | Gini 0.06–0.21. In Neurology one doctor did 71% of the appointments. |
| 09 | Are patients with earlier misses more likely to miss again? | Point-in-time features (no look-ahead), lift against overall | 2+ prior misses: 18.8% not completed (lift 1.56). No prior misses: 6.7% (lift 0.56). |
| 10 | Where do patients go next after a visit to each department? | `LEAD` per patient, transition matrix, suppression under 5 | Cardiology patients' next visit was General Medicine 21.8% of the time and Cardiology again 10.7%. |
| 11 | When were departments booked beyond their slots? | Daily operations mart, pressure ratio, conditional counts | No overbooked days. The hospital app prevents double booking, so this query mostly serves as a data check. |
| 12 | How full are the next four weeks compared with last year? | `generate_series` weeks, pre-aggregated self-join 52 weeks back | Next week ranged from 0.72× (Cardiology) to 1.38× (ENT) of the same week last year. Optimised from 339 ms to 54 ms. |
| 13 | How many bookings disappear before they happen? | Removal rate by department and month | 12 removals out of 26,339 seen, because the pipeline was only a few runs old. The query undercounts by design until the pipeline has run for a while. |
| 14 | What share of each month's patients are new? | `MIN() OVER (PARTITION BY patient)`, suppression | About 2% new patients per month in 2026. The first months of data are nearly all "new" (left-censoring), as the header warns. |

## Conventions used in every query

* **Removed appointments** are excluded from demand counts (`NOT is_removed`) unless the query is about removals.
* **Past outcomes** use only `completed` and `unresolved`. Upcoming appointments have no outcome yet.
* **Privacy.** Any count of patients under 5 is blanked (`CASE WHEN n >= 5`), or the row is dropped with `HAVING`. Patients are counted by surrogate key only.
* **No look-ahead.** Anything about "before" uses window frames that end at `1 PRECEDING`.
* **Validation.** Each header names a check that ties the result back to a simpler count. For example, query 01's monthly totals must add up to `COUNT(*)` of non-removed appointments.

## Performance

On the synthetic database (26,339 appointments), every query runs in under 70 ms. The full list is in [sql-plans/README.md](sql-plans/README.md).

The one slow query was 12, which joined raw appointment rows with an `IN (…)` condition. It now counts appointments per week and department first, then joins, which cut the time by about six times. Nothing needed an index on the hospital tables. The warehouse's own indexes cover the access paths: `fact_appointment (date_key)`, `(department_sk, date_key)`, `(doctor_sk, date_key)` and `(patient_sk, date_key)`, plus `fact_doctor_daily_capacity (department_sk, date_key)`.
